About

Hi — I'm Edward.

I like the part of this work most people find annoying: something is broken, nobody knows why, and you have to work backwards until it makes sense. That instinct turned into a cybersecurity degree from the University at Albany, a summer on the front line of a hospital help desk, and a Linux server at home that I have been quietly keeping alive for a couple of years.

That server is where most of what I actually know came from. Not from tutorials — from a backup that didn't restore, a service that died quietly and stayed dead for two days before I noticed, a certificate that expired at the worst possible moment. Everything on this site is built the same way: stand it up, break it on purpose, fix it, then write it down well enough that I could rebuild it from the notes.

The other half of the job is people. I'm patient with someone who is frustrated with their computer, I explain things without making anyone feel stupid, and I would rather ask a question than guess and be wrong twice.

Right now I'm looking for a first full-time role in desktop or IT support, and studying for CompTIA Network+.

Now / Learning

Studying for CompTIA Network+.

Alongside it I'm working through the gap between "I built this once" and "I could run this for someone else." Recent things that changed how I work:

  • An untested backup is not a backup. The restore drill in the backup lab is logged for a reason — the first one I ran didn't work.
  • The ticket is the deliverable. Fixing it is half the job; the other half is a note the next person can actually use. The helpdesk lab has five worked tickets and the KB article each one produced.
  • Give out the smallest permission that works. The AD lab delegates password resets to a helpdesk group and nothing else — the boundary I'd be working inside on day one.
  • Write it down while it hurts. Every runbook here exists because I'd already had to rediscover the same fix twice.
